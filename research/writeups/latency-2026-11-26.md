# Order latency: where the seconds go from the bar close to the order (big-execution, 2026-11-26)

Why: ATLAS's 3 real entries filled 9 / 11 / 15 ticks worse than the model (research/writeups/execpol-2026-11-26.md), and ATLAS
needs ≤ 3 ticks to keep its 2021-25 edge. If those ticks are paid while the order is on its way, a faster send saves them
with no missed trades. This branch measures the chain, makes the latency visible per order, and adds the faster transport as
an opt-in. **No decision changed** (proof at the end). Paper rules unchanged; nothing arms, sizes or routes differently by default.

## The chain today (from the code + a replay of Nov 6 / 17 / 18 with the real LiveBot)
`research/latency/chain.py` runs research/speed/replay_harness.py: the real engine.live.LiveBot, paper adapter, frozen clock,
LE_BarFeed-format files growing bar by bar, LIT + ATLAS (account B). 2,304 polls, 0 errors. It reproduced ATLAS's real
Nov 6 04:00 and Nov 17 08:20 sells (the Nov 18 MACD entry is not taken in this eval-stage replay).

| Step | Seconds after the 5m bar opens (ATLAS) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Bar closes; NinjaTrader writes the closed bar + the new minute's open on the **first trade** of the new minute | ~0.05 (RTH) to ~0.5 (thin night) | LE_BarFeed / LE_Feed (OnBarClose) |
| 2. **The poll wakes: a fixed 2.0 s after the minute** (engine/live.next_wake) | **2.0** | by design: "+2 s is a safe guess both files are there" |
| 3. Poll work before ATLAS: bars + merge (~145 ms), LIT model (~139 ms), LIT route, guards, save, BLITZ, Brain | +0.30 | replay stages at 5m polls |
| 4. ATLAS: engine.second.run on the 3 members at the 5m open (median 230-330 ms), sizing, guards -> order line | +0.30 | replay: order handed to the adapter at **597 / 635 ms** into the poll |
| 5. Order file -> NinjaTrader pickup -> Tradovate -> CME | +0.05-0.15 (more on OneDrive) | speed study 2026-11-20; ack wait is checked every 50 ms |
| **Order at the exchange** | **≈ 2.7-2.8 s** | the ledger's 3-5 s stamp comes after fill + bracket confirm |

**About 75% of it (2.0 s) is the fixed wait; ~0.6 s is the bot's own poll; ~0.1 s NinjaTrader.** LIT is the same minus step 4
(order at ~2.4 s). The cloud machine is not the PC: the PC's polls were slower in October (5-32 s, fixed since: background work
runs in a worker process), so the PC's step 3-4 may be larger. The new log (below) measures it on the PC.

## Before / after (estimate)
| Setup | Order reaches CME after the bar opens | Gain |
|---|---|---|
| Today | ≈ 2.7-2.8 s | |
| + **fast entry ON** (`python -X utf8 -m engine.fast_entry --on` on the PC) | ≈ 0.8-1.1 s (wake at first tick + 0.15 s, then the same 0.6 s poll) | **-1.7 to -1.9 s** |
| + socket transport (Sim only until the paper week passes) | ≈ 0.7-1.0 s | -0.05 to -0.15 s |
| + ATLAS fast lane (proposal, NOT built: route ATLAS first at a 5m open) | ≈ 0.4-0.6 s | -0.3 s, see the risk below |
| + incremental engine.second.run (proposal) | ≈ 0.2-0.4 s | -0.2 s |

**What fast entry is worth (honest range).** If the 12 ticks build up like √time over the 2.75 s: 2.75 → 1.0 s saves ~4.8
ticks a trade. Linear: ~7.6. All of it on the very first trade: 0. At ATLAS's live size (2 micros, ~15 trades a month) that is
**$0 to ~$115 a month**. On the long run it decides whether ATLAS keeps an edge: 2021-25 needs ≤ 3.1 ticks of entry cost.
LIT: ~1 tick, worth little. The 2026-11-20 speed study kept fast entry OFF because its low end is $0, and that is still true.
The new log settles it in a few weeks: `send_t - signal_t` and the fill vs the 1m open, per ATLAS entry, ON vs OFF.

**Recommendation: switch fast entry ON on the PC** (one command, any time, takes effect within 30 s, decisions identical,
proved on 2,304 polls in the 2026-11-20 study and again by test_lit_live here). Keep the socket transport OFF until its Sim
week (execution-2026-11-26). Do not build the ATLAS fast lane before a red team: routing ATLAS before LIT changes which
of the two is blocked when both fire opposite ways in the same minute (the no-hedging rule checks the other's position), a
decision change in that rare case.
