# The website business for local businesses: what pays in 2026, the offers most people miss, and how a solo owner starts

day-web-research, 2026-11-20. Research only: public web sources, cited at the bottom. Most price figures come from vendors who sell the
thing (hosting companies, agencies, 3D software makers), so every number here is a range and an ESTIMATE. The short brief the build teams
use is `research/handoff/2026-11-20/web/research/RESEARCH.md`.

## The answer in five lines
1. Do not sell "a website". Sell **a monthly engine that brings calls** (site + Google profile + reviews + missed-call text-back), with the
   site as the first step. Retainers compound; one-off builds stop when selling stops.
2. Use **3D where people buy a thing or a place** (venues, rooms, products, vehicles, sheds), never as decoration. Use free tools:
   phone scans (Gaussian splats) and Google's `<model-viewer>`. That turns a $15,000-$75,000 agency product into a $1,500-$3,500 one.
3. The first door-opener is **free proof about their own business**: a speed + accessibility + Google-profile check, or a 3D scan of their
   room shown on your phone.
4. Fixed packages, 2 revision rounds, extras priced up front. Scope creep is the top killer of small web shops.
5. Realistic target: 10 retainer clients at about $250 a month = **about $2,500 a month**, plus builds. Estimated 6-12 months part time.

## 2. Market facts that sell (use them on the pitch page)
- **17%** of US small businesses still have no website (Clutch, Aug 2025, 406 owners). Top reason: "not relevant to my industry" (34%).
- **97%** of consumers read reviews of local businesses (BrightLocal 2026). In 2025, 83% used Google for reviews; by 2026 Google 71% and
  Apple Maps 27%: profiles on BOTH matter. 88% would use a business that replies to all its reviews (2024).
- **ADA website suits**: about 3,100-3,900 federal in 2025 (+23-27%), over 5,000 with state courts; about 2 in 3 against companies under
  $50M revenue (Level Access, UsableNet, Clym). Never promise "lawsuit-proof"; say "fixes the common problems".
- **3D on product pages**: Shopify reports merchants who add 3D see a 94% conversion lift on average (its own data, no method shown).
  Shed makers with a configurator report double the online leads (IdeaRoom, ShedPro: vendors).
- **3D menus**: peer-reviewed evidence is weak (WSU 2026: more interest to visit and share, about +0.5 on a scale; TU Delft field test:
  no higher order value). Do not lead with restaurants.
- **Venue tours**: claims of +18% to +47% bookings exist but all come from tour sellers (Rowton Hall case, Best Western quote). Use as
  "venues report", never as a promise.

## 4. How the money stacks (a realistic year, ESTIMATE)
- Month 1-3: 4 starter sites ($6,000) + 3 call engines ($900/mo) + 1 scan ($500).
- Month 4-6: +3 call engines, +2 care plans, 1 product viewer ($2,500). Retainers about $1,950/mo.
- Month 7-12: +4 retainers, 2 configurator-lites ($7,000). Retainers about $3,000/mo by month 12.
- Costs: domains about $12 a year a client; hosting free (Cloudflare Pages / GitHub Pages) or $5-$25/mo; review / text-back tools $29-$75 a
  client a month (pass them through); Scaniverse free; model-viewer free (Apache-2.0). Set aside about 30% for tax (self-employed).

## 5. First 5 clients for free (the owner does every call and email by hand)
1. Build the list from free public data: businesses on maps with 20+ good reviews but no website, a broken one, or a slow one
   (leads team). Business listings only, no private people.
2. Before contacting, build a **demo of their business** (their name, their real photos, their reviews): the demo is the pitch. For venues,
   a 3D scan on your phone after a 10-minute visit.
3. Offer the first 2 clients a founding price (the cost of tools) for a written review, a case-study page and 2 referrals.
4. Channels that cost $0: walking in at quiet hours; local Facebook / Nextdoor business groups (answer questions, do not spam); chambers of
   commerce and BNI visits as a guest; asking every client "who else do you know who needs this?".
5. Rules: no auto-dialing or cold texting (TCPA); cold emails to business addresses must carry a real address and an opt-out (CAN-SPAM);
   never scrape personal data.

## 6. What to automate (Claude builds once, reuses forever)
- A site template per trade (plumber, salon, venue, maker) with a 1-page brief form -> a first draft in an hour.
- The free audit report (speed, mobile, accessibility basics, Google profile completeness) -> PDF / page with plain-$ advice.
- Proposal + contract generator with the fixed packages, revision rounds and price list (anti scope creep).
- Monthly proof report per client (calls, reviews, visits).
- Review-request message templates and reply drafts (the owner approves each reply).

## 7. What kills small web agencies (and the guard for each)
| Killer | Guard |
|---|---|
| Scope creep ("one more change") | Fixed packages, 2 revision rounds, a written change price |
| Only one-off builds | Every build includes a monthly plan; target 70% of revenue from retainers |
| Clients who cannot see results, then cancel | The monthly proof report (#13) |
| Custom work that takes weeks | Templates + free 3D tools; no bespoke 3D until 5 retainers |
| Chasing clients who cannot pay | Qualify: open 2+ years, has reviews, has a phone line; 50% up front |
| Hosting lock-in fights | Client owns the domain; written handover at the end of the term |
| Promising rankings or legal compliance | Promise work done, report results; never "guaranteed #1" or "ADA compliant" |
