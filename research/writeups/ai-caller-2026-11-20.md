Team day-caller. **Not legal advice.** Statute text below is quoted from the Maryland General Assembly's own pages; everything else is
from law-firm, vendor and FCC pages read on 2026-11-20. Before the first live dial, one hour with a TCPA lawyer (about $300-$500) is
the cheapest insurance there is next to a $500-$1,500-per-call lawsuit.

## 2. The law, with sources

### Maryland: Stop the Spam Calls Act (Md. Code, Commercial Law 14-4501 to 14-4503, in force 2024-01-01)

Read on the Maryland General Assembly statute pages (mgaleg.maryland.gov, sections 14-4501, 14-4502, 14-4503; Public Utilities 8-205):

- **The ban, 14-4502(a)(3):** "Without the prior express written consent of the called party, a person may not make or cause to be
  made" a telephone solicitation that involves (i) "An automated system for the selection or dialing of telephone numbers" or (ii) "The
  playing of a recorded message when a connection is completed to the number called."
- **Businesses are covered.** "Called party" is "a person who is a regular user of a telephone number that receives a telephone
  solicitation" (14-4501(b)), and 14-4503(b) presumes a call to a Maryland area code reaches someone who resides "or [has] a place of
  business in the State". "Telephone solicitation" (Public Utilities 8-205) is an organized campaign by phone to sell goods or services.
- **AI voices:** the Act never says "AI". But its consent definition (14-4501(d)) covers "the playing of a recorded or artificial voice
  message", and an AI caller is software that picks and dials numbers by itself, which is "an automated system for the selection or
  dialing" in plain words (Maryland did not copy the narrow federal autodialer test). We plan as if it is covered.
- **The B2B exemption is too narrow for us, 14-4502(a)(1)(iii):** "A business-to-business sale where the telephone solicitor has been
  lawfully operating continuously for at least 3 years" "under the same business name". Other exemptions: an isolated transaction (i),
  "A single telephone solicitation made to a customer or client in response to an inquiry or request" (v), and an existing customer whose
  informational call turns into a sale at their request (vi).
- **What consent must look like, 14-4501(d):** a written agreement that "Bears the signature of the called party", clearly authorises
  the automated dialing / artificial voice, names the number, and says signing is not a condition of buying. Our form does exactly that
  (`tools/caller/consent.py`, wording `v1-2026-11-20`).
- **Also in 14-4502(c), for every sales call:** not between 8 pm and 8 am their time, at most 3 calls in 24 hours on the same subject,
  never a disguised voice to hide who is calling.
- **Money, 14-4503(a):** a violation is an unfair or deceptive trade practice under the Maryland Consumer Protection Act; the person
  called can sue for "damages up to $500 or actual damages", and up to three times that if willful or knowing.

So: cold AI calls to Maryland businesses = illegal for us until the agency has 3 years under one name (late 2029 at the earliest, and
even then only for true B2B sales). Calls the business asked for, with a signed form, are fine.

### Federal

- **FCC Declaratory Ruling FCC 24-17 (2024-02-08):** AI-generated voices are "artificial" voices under the TCPA (47 U.S.C. 227).
- **The TCPA itself** needs prior express (written, for sales) consent for artificial-voice calls to **cell phones** (any purpose, B2B
  included) and to **residential lines**. A true business landline is outside 227(b)(1)(B), which is why the federal law alone would not
  stop us; Maryland does. A one-person business on a cell phone is a cell phone: never.
- **Identification (47 CFR 64.1200(b)):** an artificial-voice call must state at the beginning who is calling and give a phone number.
  Our opener says it in the first sentence; the callback number is in every email and on the consent form.
- **E-SIGN:** the FCC's 2012 TCPA order (77 FR 34233, 2012-06-11) accepts consent signed electronically (a web form with a typed name
  and a tap). That is how the demo-page form counts as "signed".
- **FTC Telemarketing Sales Rule:** most B2B calls are exempt, but since 2024-05-16 B2B calls must not misrepresent anything.
- **Do Not Call:** business numbers are outside the National Do Not Call Registry (FTC FAQ), but we scrub anyway as you asked (5 area
  codes free) and keep our own list forever.
- **Pending:** the FCC's August 2024 proposal on AI-call disclosure (Federal Register 2024-19028) was not final as of our search; we
  already do what it proposes (say it is AI at the start).
- **Courts since 2025:** *McLaughlin Chiropractic v. McKesson* (US Supreme Court, 2025-06-20) means judges no longer have to follow FCC
  readings of the TCPA. That cuts both ways; we follow the strictest reading.

### Other states (for leads outside Maryland)

- **Pennsylvania, Act 47 of 2026 (SB 992, signed 2026-07-20, in force 2026-10-18):** robocalls to residential, **business** or wireless
  lines need written consent; calls only 9 am-7 pm Mon-Sat, never Sundays or holidays. Consent-first passes; our 9-5 weekday window is
  inside theirs.
- Florida, Oklahoma and others have their own mini-TCPAs (consent + 8 am-8 pm + 3 calls/24 h). Consent-first plus our 9-5 weekday window
  passes all the ones in `docs/COLD_CALLING_RULES.md`.

### Recording

Maryland is an all-party-consent state for recording (Courts & Judicial Proceedings 10-402). The first sentence of every call says it is
recorded and transcribed, before the business says anything, and the consent form says it too. Anyone who does not want that can hang up
or say stop.

## 3. What the caller does on a call

1. **First sentence, built in code (never by the AI):** "Hi, this is Ava, an AI assistant calling for <agency>, and this call is
   recorded and transcribed. Is this <business>? You asked us to call about the free website preview we made for you, is now an OK time
   for two minutes?" The code refuses to start a call if that sentence does not say AI + agency + recorded.
2. **Talks like a person, never pretends to be one.** "Is this a robot?" -> "I'll be straight with you: I'm an AI assistant, not a
   person. <owner> is the real person..." Any reply that claims to be human is replaced before it is spoken (tested with a model that lies).
3. **Handles the usual:** busy (offers to email + one more try another day), "how much?" (the price line, then a call with you),
   "my nephew does sites" (emails the finished preview anyway), "we already have one" (side-by-side link), two no's = polite goodbye.
4. **Books:** offers 2 slots from the settings (next business day 10:00, the one after 14:00; skips holidays), asks for an email,
   books, queues the confirmation + preview email, **one phone ping per booking**. Ready to buy -> emails the payment link.
5. **"Don't call me"** at any point: apology, call ends that turn, number on our do-not-call list and its consent revoked, forever.

Simulation (8 scripted business owners, offline): every persona 100% on disclosure, honesty, opt-out, no pushing and the right
outcome; 3 booked, 1 demo emailed, 1 callback, 1 opt-out, 2 polite declines. Transcripts:
`research/handoff/2026-11-20/caller/transcripts/`.

## 4. The compliance gate (in code, tested)

Before every dial, `tools/caller/compliance.py` checks, and logs every answer with the call:
ON switch (typed phrase) - daily cap (max 100) - signed consent for exactly that number (< 365 days, current wording, not revoked) - our
do-not-call list - the federal registry copy (area code downloaded, <= 31 days old) - **line type = landline** (Twilio Lookup; mobile,
VoIP, toll-free, unknown = never) - Mon-Fri 9:00-16:55 their time, no federal holidays, no day after Thanksgiving - at most 1 call + 1
follow-up, the follow-up only after no answer / busy / voicemail / "call me back" and 2+ business days later. Answering machines: it
hangs up, never leaves an AI voicemail. 93 checks in `tests/test_caller.py`.

## 6. Your sign-up steps (20 minutes, then only passwords)

1. **Twilio** (twilio.com, "Sign up"): verify your email and your own cell. Upgrade the account (card; pay-as-you-go, no monthly fee).
   Buy one **local number** with a 301 or 240 area code ($1.15/month, Voice). In Console -> Account -> API keys & tokens, copy the
   **Account SID** and **Auth Token**.
2. **Claude API** (platform.claude.com): sign in, add $10 credit, Settings -> API keys -> Create key. Copy it.
3. **Do-Not-Call registry** (telemarketing.donotcall.gov): register as a seller (free for 5 area codes), download 301, 240, 410, 443,
   667, save them as `%USERPROFILE%\.olympus\caller\registry\301.txt` etc., run `python -m tools.caller.registry`. Every 31 days again.
4. **Tailscale Funnel** (already on your PC): in the Tailscale admin console, allow Funnel for this PC (Access controls). It gives
   Twilio a public address only for the caller's port 10000 (it is never shown on a public page).
   **Cloudflare** (you already have it for the site): Workers & Pages -> KV -> create a namespace "CONSENT"; then a token with
   "Workers KV Storage: Edit". CALLER_SETUP.bat asks for the account id, namespace id and that token (optional keys 5-7).
5. Double-click **CALLER_SETUP.bat**, paste the 4 keys (nothing shows on screen; saved only in `%USERPROFILE%\.olympus\caller.json`).
6. Fill your agency name, phone and address in `agency\config.json` (the AI must say who it calls for; it refuses to start otherwise).
7. When a business has signed the form: **CALLER_ON.bat**, type **START CALLING** and a daily cap. **CALLER_STOP.bat** stops it.

## 7. Open points (honest)

- The Maryland Act does not define "automated system" or mention AI; a court could read it more narrowly. We do not bet on that.
- The concept pages have the form (day-cities3). It posts to the site's own Cloudflare address (free), never to your PC, and your PC collects the signed forms from there every minute. Two setup items remain: the publisher deploys that small function, and your real agency name and phone go into the consent text. Until then only inbound calls happen.
- Emails go out through the email autopilot (day-a11y): the caller writes its `outbox/<id>.json` drafts; the PC pushes them to the private agency-inbox branch and sends them.
- ConversationRelay voice and timing were not tried on a real phone line (no keys, no money spent). The first live test should be the
  owner calling the agency number from his own phone.
