# Taxes for a prop-firm trader (US, 2026 rules): study notes, 2026-11-17

Education, not tax advice. Federal rules only; the owner's state is unknown. Checked on 2026-11-17 against IRS.gov first,
then the firms' own help centers. Where the law is unsettled, it says so. Page: `tools/web_public/business/taxes.html`.

## In one minute
- MFFU payouts are pay for a service (trading a simulated account), not gains on your own money. They are normally
  self-employment income: Schedule C (profit or loss) plus Schedule SE (self-employment tax). No tax is withheld.
- Self-employment tax is 15.3% (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare) on 92.35% of net profit, about 14.1% of profit.
  Income tax comes on top.
- Eval fees, resets, data, platform, a business share of the PC and internet, and a home office if it qualifies are
  business costs that lower the profit.
- Pay quarterly (Form 1040-ES). Next due: **Jan 15, 2027** (for Sep-Dec 2026), then Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, 2027.
- **Set-aside rule: move 30% of every payout into a separate tax pot the day it lands.** Pay the quarterly tax from it.

## 1. How the payouts are taxed
- **The form.** MFFU's help center ("Your 1099 tax documents", dated 2026-03-04) says Rise payouts get their 1099 from
  Rise's tax department, mailed to the address on the Rise account; Plaid/ACH payouts get it from MFFU at the registered
  mailing address (the 2025 forms were due by Feb 17, 2026). The page does not name the 1099 type. For a payment for
  services it is normally a **1099-NEC**; check the box number when it arrives.
- **The threshold.** For payments made after Dec 31, 2025 the 1099-NEC / 1099-MISC reporting threshold rose from $600 to
  **$2,000** (P.L. 119-21, the 2025 tax law; IRS 1099-NEC FAQ, Pub 1099 2026). Small years may get no form at all.
  **The income is taxable whether or not a form arrives.**
- **Where it goes.** Schedule C (Form 1040), then Schedule SE (IRS Self-Employed Individuals Tax Center).
- **SE tax.** 15.3% = 12.4% Social Security (on the first $184,500 of combined wages and self-employment earnings in 2026)
  + 2.9% Medicare; an extra 0.9% Medicare above $200,000 (single). Owed when net SE earnings are $400 or more. Half of it
  (the "employer-equivalent portion") is deducted when figuring adjusted gross income. The 92.35% factor is on Schedule SE
  (net profit x 0.9235 is the SE earnings base). Source: IRS "Self-employment tax (Social Security and Medicare taxes)".
- **Not 60/40.** Section 1256 (60% long-term / 40% short-term for futures) is for gains on contracts you hold. A prop
  payout is the firm paying you; you never owned the positions. The common reading, and what the 1099-NEC implies, is
  ordinary self-employment income. (Some traders argue otherwise; it is not the safe default.)
- **Losing years.** If fees exceed payouts in a year and the trading is run as a real business (records, a profit
  motive), the Schedule C loss can offset other income. Fees paid before the business really starts may instead be
  start-up costs (up to $5,000 deductible in the first year, the rest spread over 15 years). Ask a pro which applies.

## 2. What can be deducted (Schedule C, ordinary and necessary business costs)
| Cost | Notes |
|---|---|
| Eval fees (Builder 50K $91.80, 25K $63 / $43), resets, activation fees | Direct costs of earning the payouts. Keep the receipt email. |
| Market data, NinjaTrader / platform licence, VPS, ntfy etc. | The business share only if also used privately. |
| The PC | Business-use share. Under the de minimis safe harbor an item up to $2,500 can be expensed at once; otherwise depreciation / Section 179. |
| Internet, phone | Business-use share only (e.g. 40% if 40% of the use is the bots). Write down how you picked the %. |
| Home office | Only a space used **regularly and exclusively** for the business. Simplified method: $5 per square foot, max 300 sq ft ($1,500). Otherwise Form 8829 with actual costs. |
| Courses, books about trading or running the agency | When they improve the business you already run. |
| Agency (if started): domain, hosting, software, ads | Same Schedule C or a second one; same SE tax. |
Not deductible: personal living costs, the share of the PC / internet used privately, fines.

## 3. Quarterly estimated tax (Form 1040-ES)
- **Who.** Individuals who expect to owe **$1,000 or more** when they file (IRS "Estimated taxes").
- **2026 due dates (Pub 505 2026):** Apr 15, 2026; Jun 15, 2026; Sep 15, 2026; **Jan 15, 2027** (covers Sep 1-Dec 31).
  If you file the 2026 return and pay everything by Jan 31, 2027, the Jan 15 payment is not needed.
- **2027 due dates** (the same IRS schedule; confirm on the 2027 Form 1040-ES when it is posted): Apr 15, 2027 (also the
  2026 return deadline); Jun 15, 2027; Sep 15, 2027; Jan 18, 2028 (Jan 15, 2028 is a Saturday and Jan 17 is MLK Day).
  A date on a weekend or legal holiday moves to the next business day.
- **Safe harbor (no underpayment penalty)** if what you paid on time is at least the smaller of **90% of this year's tax**
  or **100% of last year's tax**; **110%** of last year's tax if last year's AGI was over $150,000 ($75,000 married filing
  separately) (Pub 505, "Higher income taxpayers"). There is also no penalty if you owed no tax last year (a full 12-month
  year as a US citizen or resident). Simplest habit: pay a quarter of last year's total tax each due date, or pay what
  is in the 30% pot.
- **How to pay, free:** IRS Direct Pay or your IRS online account (pick "estimated tax, 1040-ES" and the right year), or
  mail the 1040-ES voucher.

## 4. QBI (Section 199A)
- Sole proprietors can deduct up to **20% of qualified business income** (IRS "Qualified business income deduction"),
  limited to 20% of taxable income before the deduction. Made permanent by the 2025 law; from 2026 a small minimum
  deduction for active QBI also exists (IRS page: "MDA-QBI"). Form 8995 (simple) or 8995-A.
- **Does it apply to prop payouts?** Probably, but not certain. Trading for your own account in securities or
  commodities is a "specified service" (SSTB) under Section 199A; whether a payout for trading a firm's sim account counts
  as "trading" is not settled. Below the income threshold (around $200,000 single for 2026; check the Form 8995
  instructions) an SSTB still gets the full deduction, so for this owner's income range it likely does not matter.
- It lowers **income tax only**, not self-employment tax.

## 5. Kalshi (paused, but for the record)
- Kalshi's help center ("What tax documentation does Kalshi provide?"): users who meet IRS thresholds get a **1099-INT**
  (interest on cash), **1099-MISC** (credits and rewards), **1099-B** (broker proceeds such as crypto transfers) or
  **1099-DA** (digital assets, via ZeroHash). Forms are under Account -> Tax Info, some by email from Zenwork. Kalshi
  computes P&L first-in-first-out and offers a yearly P&L download (CSV).
- **The trading profit itself is usually not on a 1099**, and **the IRS has issued no guidance on how event-contract
  gains are taxed.** Positions in tax guides range from capital gain/loss, to Section 1256 60/40 (disputed: event contracts
  are not clearly "regulated futures contracts"), to ordinary or gambling-like income. **This is uncertain.** The choice
  changes whether losses offset gains, so a pro should decide before a year with real Kalshi profit. Keep the yearly CSV.
- It is **not** Schedule C / SE income unless it is run as a business, and it is separate from the MFFU income.

## 6. State tax
Unknown state. Most states tax this income too and many want their own quarterly payments. Nine have no personal income
tax: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, Wyoming (New Hampshire's business
profits tax and Washington's B&O tax can still reach a sole proprietor above their size thresholds). Check the
state's department of revenue site. The 30% pot is meant to cover a typical state tax as well.

## 7. Records to keep (3 years after filing, IRS "How long should I keep records")
- Every payout: date, account, gross, split, net received; the Rise / bank statement.
- Every eval fee, reset, activation, data or platform receipt.
- PC receipt and the business-use %; internet / phone bills and the %; home office size and photo.
- The 1099s; each estimated payment's confirmation number; last year's return (for the safe harbor).
- Kalshi yearly P&L CSV and any 1099s.
- In this repo: the Ledger (`tools/web_public/ledger/`) and Oikos already hold fees and payouts; the Business page's
  tax-year CSV (`tools/business.py`) is the starting file for a preparer.

## 8. Get help free
- **VITA** (IRS Volunteer Income Tax Assistance): sites set their own limits, generally for people earning about
  **$70,000 or less** (the IRS page has the exact figure); some sites do not do Schedule C with losses or many expenses, so ask when booking. Locator on IRS.gov
  or 800-906-9887. **TCE / AARP Tax-Aide** for 60+.
- **IRS Free File** (guided software) if income qualifies.
- Above that, a paid preparer or enrolled agent once a year is usually worth it for a Schedule C with quarterly payments.
  (The rule here is "no paid services" for the bots; a tax preparer is the owner's own choice.)

## 9. The set-aside rule
**30% of every payout, moved to a separate account the day it arrives.** Pay the quarterly tax from that pot. Whatever
is left in the pot after the return is filed is yours.

Why 30%: a rough 2026 federal estimate (single, standard deduction $16,100, no other income) on $2,800/month net
($33,600/yr) is SE tax ~$4,747 + income tax ~$1,210 (after half-SE and QBI) = **~$5,960, about 18%**. With a day job in
the 22% bracket the same $33,600 costs about **30%** (SE ~$4,747 + income tax ~$5,500). 30% covers that case, plus most
state taxes, without guessing the owner's situation. If he has no other income and no state tax, 20-25% would do; that is
for a tax pro to confirm. Oikos and the Ledger have a slider to change it.

Worked examples:
- **$1,000 payout:** $300 to the tax pot, **$700 yours**.
- **$2,800 a month:** $840 a month to the pot ($10,080 a year), **$1,960 a month yours**. About $2,520 per quarter is
  ready on each due date.

**Alignment.** Oikos (`oikoscore.js`, `tools/money/oikos.py`) and the Ledger (`ledgercore.js`) defaulted to 25%; the
Business fixture said "25-30%". All defaults are now 30% (a saved choice on the phone is kept).
