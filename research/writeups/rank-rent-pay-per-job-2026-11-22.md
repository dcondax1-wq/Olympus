Honest read: neither new idea beats the website sale in the first 90 days. Pay-per-lead is the only one worth a small test, and mainly as a **closing tool** ("$0 up front, pay only per call") that makes the $1,500 objection go away.

## 1. Rank-and-rent

**How it works:** build a site (+ ideally a Google profile) for "roof repair <town>", rank it, rent the phone number to one contractor per town.

**What rent really is (UNVERIFIED for MD):** no source gives a rent figure for roofers. Closest anchors: small-market roofing SEO retainers quoted at $750-$1,500/mo and "real" roofing SEO $1,500-$5,000/mo (agency pages, so inflated); pay-per-lead subscription starters at $85-$250/mo (Hook Agency, Alvon, WebFX listings). A buyer will compare rent to what 1-2 leads cost them ($100-$264 exclusive each). So the **supportable rent is about the value of 3-6 exclusive leads: ~$300-$900/mo**. My table uses $450 middle, $800 good. HVAC/roofing higher, plumbing/electrical lower (Service Direct lists electricians $55-$175 a lead, plumbing $60-$255, AC $65-$325).

**Time to rank (be skeptical):** sources say "3+ months" for any movement, "8 months" for one zero-to-hyperlocal case study (Whitespark MozCon 2019); no source gives a failure rate. For a brand-new domain against established MD contractors with reviews, plan **6-9 months to first stable calls, 12+ in the competitive towns**, and some towns never. I found no failure-rate data (UNVERIFIED).

**Competition:** 20k-150k MD towns (Gaithersburg 69,657, Rockville 67,117, Bowie 58,329, Hagerstown 43,527, Westminster 20,126 per 2020 Census QuickFacts; Frederick, Annapolis, Salisbury likewise in range) in the DC/Baltimore corridor are packed: Angi/Yelp/Thumbtack, Local Services Ads, and contractors with hundreds of reviews. Not verified town by town; the 14-day test below checks 3 towns for free.

**Google rules (the dealbreaker):**
- Google's eligibility guideline lists **lead generation agents or companies as ineligible** for a Business Profile. Disclosing call routing does not fix it (secondary source).
- Service-area businesses must hide a street address; PO boxes and unstaffed virtual offices are not allowed; a storefront must be staffed in its listed hours.
- So a profile we create and rent out is the exact thing that gets suspended. Without it we are organic-only, and "roof repair <town>" calls come mostly from the Maps pack.
- Safer variant: the contractor owns the profile and we only build the site, which is just our normal website sale.

**Maryland contractor-advertising rule:** MHIC says every home improvement ad must carry the **contractor's name and license number** (websites, social, email, etc.; vehicles need the number at least 3 in tall per a dated bulletin). If our rented site speaks as the contractor it needs their name + "MHIC No." and a truthful non-misleading claim; if it speaks as a referral service it must say so and not imply it is licensed. Only MHIC-licensed contractors may contract with homeowners; our role must be lead supplier, never the one who sells the job (a salesperson license is needed to negotiate or seek contracts - Bus. Reg. 8-101). Ask a Maryland attorney before launch (UNVERIFIED whether a pure lead site needs any license).

**Call tracking cost:** CallRail entry ~$50-$55/mo (5 numbers, 250 minutes) and tiers up to ~$215 (Capterra/Findstack/Software Advice; sites disagree). A cheap competitor lists extra numbers at ~$3/mo, toll-free $5 (CloudTalk blog, may be dated). One number per town ~ $36/yr; CallRail-grade tracking for 10 towns = $600+/yr. **Maryland is an all-party-consent state for recording calls** (Courts & Jud. Proc. 10-402): never record without a spoken notice and consent; the legislature was reworking this (status UNVERIFIED).

Middle case = ~$25 per owner hour in year 1. Risk: renters cancel when a month is slow, and the site has no value to them without leads.

## 2. Pay-per-booked-job / pay-per-lead

**Why it fits us better:** the 2,280 high-ticket contractors have a confirmed-dead website, so they already lose clicks and calls their Google profile still generates. A working site with a tracked number can recapture some of that demand. We don't have to rank anything. But the volume is an unmeasured guess.

**What leads sell for (vendor claims, no neutral source):**
- Roofing exclusive: $80-$220 per lead; shared $15-$85 (99calls, ActiveProspect, Fixr listings). Close rate exclusive 35-41% (vendor claim), shared 8-15%.
- HVAC exclusive form leads $100-$264; qualified exclusive pay-per-lead $250-$600 (Elevarus); Google LSA average $51/lead (99calls, Valley Marketing Group); shared $25-$90.
- Service Direct pay-per-call by trade: electricians $55-$175, plumbing $60-$255, AC $65-$325.
- Angi/HomeAdvisor and Thumbtack do not publish prices; contractor-reported $15-$85 (roofing >$100), 3-4 contractors share each Angi lead. No Reddit/forum thread was found (UNVERIFIED). Thumbtack says no subscription fees.

**Typical MD job tickets:** roof replacement average $8,251 (This Old House) to $11,500-$13,225 (Askdoss / other 2026 sites), range ~$6,500-$25,000; HVAC full replacement average $8,625-$9,800, range $4,400-$17,250 (Askdoss, HVAC Project Cost). Repairs run far less ($300-$1,500). A 30%-close exclusive lead is worth ~$2,500-$3,500 of revenue to a roofer, so $100-$200 a lead is reasonable to them.

**Maryland law on referral fees / percent-of-job (UNVERIFIED; get a Maryland attorney):**
- I found nothing that bans a flat fee for a customer lead. I also found nothing authorizing it.
- Real risk: Bus. Reg. 8-101 defines a "salesperson" as someone who negotiates or seeks home improvement contracts with an owner; MHIC cites contractors for doing business "with or through an unlicensed person" (COMAR civil citation list; 8-612 title "Doing Business With Unlicensed Person"). A **percentage of the job** paid to us looks like commission for selling, so it invites the salesperson question; a **flat fee per call/appointment for advertising** is much closer to ordinary lead sales (Angi charges flat per lead).
- Our rule: **flat fee per qualified call, never % of job**, never attend or negotiate the sale, contractor alone contracts with the homeowner and puts their own MHIC No. on the contract.

**Contract wording to get checked by an attorney:** (1) we sell advertising/leads, not home improvement services, and do not negotiate or sign anything with homeowners; (2) a "qualified lead" = inbound call from the tracked number lasting 60+ seconds, new customer, in the stated towns, for a paid service (not spam/wrong number/existing customer); (3) fee fixed at $X per qualified lead, invoiced weekly, due in 7 days; (4) prepaid credit or card on file with a stated cap; (5) lead disputes within 48 hours or accepted; (6) we pause the number when unpaid over the cap; (7) the contractor's MHIC number and name displayed on the site; (8) no call recording unless disclosed and consented under MD law; (9) 30-day cancel, we keep the phone number and site.

|---|---|---|---|

Costs: tracked numbers ~$3-$5 each, or $50-$110 for a CallRail-grade account; no per-lead cost to us because the traffic is already theirs. The big UNVERIFIED number is leads per contractor per month: a contractor with a dead site might get 1-3 web calls a month, not 6. **Treat the good column as a stretch.** The middle column at 10 contractors ($2,400/mo) already equals ~10 website sales' retainers.

**Cash-flow risk and how to cap it:**
- No-pay: contractors dispute or stall ("that was a wrong number"). Cap: card on file or prepaid $100 credit; stop the number after $200 owed or 7 days late; weekly not monthly billing.
- Chargebacks: our records are tracking-number call logs with caller, date and duration; the signed agreement defines a qualified lead. Pay by ACH or card with a signed authorization.
- Lead quality fights: refund policy of 1 free replacement for non-qualifying calls, stated up front.
- Concentration: no contractor above 40% of revenue.
- Worst case we lose ~$200 per contractor and the contractor still holds the free site (we cost-cap at ~$0 since sites are already built).

## 4. The ONE recommended 14-day test (cost cap $100, no signups)

**Test: "$0 up front, pay per call" pilot against the $1,500 site, to 40 dead-site high-ticket contractors.**

1. **Days 1-2 (1.5 h, $0):** pick 40 businesses from the top of the caller list (roofing/HVAC, site confirmed down, concept site ready, phone, Maryland). Write the one-page term sheet from section 2 (flat $75 per qualified call, first 3 free, $0 setup). Have a Maryland attorney read it before any contractor signs.
2. **Days 3-10 (4-5 h, $0):** **human dialing only** (no AI calls - illegal in Maryland). Script: the existing website opener, then "or if you'd rather not pay $1,500, you can pay only when a customer actually calls you." Log per business: reached owner / said yes to the Starter / said yes to the pilot / said no / no answer.
3. **Days 8-12 (1 h, $20-$40 at most):** for every pilot yes, put up the existing concept site with one tracked number (a $3-$5/mo number, no recording). Do not buy ads, domains, or CallRail yet.
4. **Days 12-14 (1 h, $0):** free check of 3 target towns (Google "roof repair <town>", "ac repair <town>"): count how many of the top-5 are individual contractors with 50+ reviews. This is the free rank-and-rent kill-check.

**Kill rules (cost cap $0-$100; total expected spend <$50):**
- **Stop the pay-per-lead idea** if, after 40 dials with at least 15 decision-maker conversations, **fewer than 2 contractors say yes to the written pilot** by day 14.
- **Stop it later** if the live pilot sites log **fewer than 3 tracked calls total in the first 30 days** (volume too low for $75 a call to be worth the hours).
- **Kill rank-and-rent for good** if in all 3 checked towns 3+ of the top 5 are established contractors with 50+ reviews (expected), or any plan needs a Google Business Profile we create for a lead-gen.
- **Keep the website sale as the default** whenever a prospect says yes to the $1,500 Starter: it pays more per hour, so count it as a win, not a failure.

**What number means go:** 2+ written pilot yeses AND 3+ tracked calls in 30 days at the first contractor = move to 10 contractors. Otherwise stay on website retainers.
