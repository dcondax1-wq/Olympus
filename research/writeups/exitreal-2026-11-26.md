# Exit + backtest realism: are our $ numbers too rosy on the exits? (big-exitreal, 2026-11-26)

PAPER RESEARCH ONLY. Pre-registered in `research/exitreal/DESIGN.md` (5eb23e12, committed before any result). Code:
`research/exitreal/exitreal.py` -> `results.json`. No strategy, size, cost constant or live file changed.

## The answer
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Do our backtests fill targets on a touch that a real limit would miss? | **No, not in our history.** Of 298 target exits (LIT 75, ATLAS 223), **0 were touch-only**. All but 6 traded **3+ ticks through** the target on the exit bar; 6 ATLAS 2021-25 targets did 1-2 ticks. A "needs 1 tick through" rule changes **$0**, and 2 ticks changes almost nothing. Same finding as lit-live-fills for LIT (Oct 18) |
| Stops: gap risk? | Rare: **1** LIT stop and **0** ATLAS stops had a bar that opened through the stop |
| What does move the numbers? | **One more tick on every stop / market exit** (2 instead of the stored 1). 2026: -0.6% to -0.7% net. 2021-25, where the edge is ~0, it flips LIT from +$861 to -$12 and costs ATLAS 17.6% |
| Eval odds (the real eval engines) | Realistic exits move pass % by **at most 0.5 points** and 6-month net by **at most -$110** (Rapid EOD 25K, H1). The stress model (2 ticks through, 4-tick stops): at most 0.9 points, -$304 |
| Pre-registered verdict | **"OPTIMISTIC" by the letter**: the 5% rule trips on 2021-25 (bases near $0). In 2026, and on every eval number, the change is inside 1%. So: **targets are modelled fairly; stop / market exits are probably 1 tick too kind**, and that only matters where the edge is thin |

## Proposal: one place for costs (`engine/costs.py`, NOT applied)
The same costs are typed in **116 files** (own constants) and **57 more** import LIT's (`research/exitreal/cost_sites.txt`).
LIT's research backtest still uses **$1.40** commission and a **1-tick** stop fill. Proposed module (one source, every engine
imports it; changing a number = one line + a re-run of the pages below):
```python
# engine/costs.py - every backtest / eval / live estimate reads these (research/writeups/exitreal-2026-11-26.md)
TICK, PV_MNQ = 0.25, 2.0
COMM_RT = 1.90           # $ per micro round turn (MFFU list price; one broker fill implies $1.82 -> keep 1.90 until 10 fills)
ENTRY_SLIP_TICKS = {"LIT": 1, "ATLAS": 12}   # measured: LIT 3 / 0 ticks (n=2), ATLAS 9 / 11 / 15 (n=3); research/batch5
STOP_SLIP_TICKS = 2      # stop-market + market exits (stored 1); E1 of this study; lit_fills' hour table + k x range for detail
TARGET_THR_TICKS = 1     # a limit target needs a trade 1 tick through (no effect on any stored trade; future-proofing)
GAP_FILLS_AT_OPEN = True # a bar opening through the stop fills near its open
```
**What would change (if adopted):** LIT's research numbers ($1.40 → $1.90 and 1 → 2-tick stops): about -1% in 2026 and to
~break-even in 2021-25. ATLAS's stored-fill numbers: -0.7% in 2026 and -17.6% in 2021-25; its money pages already use 10 ticks
and do not move. Pages to refresh: STRATEGY.md (LIT stats), docs/TESTED_AND_REJECTED.md (cost lines), docs/CLAUDE_REFERENCE.md
(costs section), the plan / fleet / forecast results (research/plans/results.json, research/forecast/forecast.json: within
±0.5 points), the hub money cards that read them, and any write-up quoting LIT's 2021-25 PF 1.01 (it becomes ~1.00).
**My recommendation:** adopt engine/costs.py with STOP_SLIP_TICKS 2 and COMM_RT 1.90 in a separate, lead-run change, since it is
pure modelling and every strategy decision stays the same. Until then, quote LIT 2021-25 as "break-even", not "slightly positive".
