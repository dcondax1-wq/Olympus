# Kalshi K7: buying "sure things" at 90-99c

## Owner summary (plain words)

**The question.** "Sure things" priced 90-99c win most of the time. Do they win often enough to pay for the price, the spread and
Kalshi's fee? I tested every category earlier teams had not touched, from 2 hours to 60 days before the market's scheduled close.
Rules were fixed on the older markets (closed before 2026-09-19) and judged once on the newest two months (2026-09-19 to 11-18).

**Verdict: no edge. Do not send any 90-99c "carry" rule live.** The newest two months were read once and every broad rule lost
money after fees. The "sure things" lose a little MORE often than their price says: at 97-99c they lose 2.2% of the time when the
price allows 1.8%; at 90-93c they lose 10.1% when 8.1% is allowed. One loss at 98c costs a full $10 bet and wipes out about 60 wins
of 17 cents. Contracts at 98-99c did lose in the held-out months (a World Series team, an AI-ranking market, a rain market,
mention words, college basketball spreads).

**The only thing that never lost** is the long-dated version: buy the side priced 97-99c a week to two months before a scheduled
event (Fed meeting strikes, Rotten Tomatoes score lines, "top AI model" lines). 98 bets, no loss, +1.6% each, in both halves. But it
is too small and too unproven to use: with zero losses in 98 bets from 42 events, the honest worst case for the loss rate is about
3% a bet (7% an event), while the break-even is 1.5%. It is about 30-60 bets a month, worth about $5-10 a month at $10 a bet, and
the money is locked for about 3 weeks a bet. One surprise (one Fed shock) erases a year of it. I did not write a candidate file.

**Interest (Kalshi help center, read 2026-11-18):** 3.50% a year, variable, paid on cash AND on open positions (valued at the day's
last traded price), accrues daily, paid monthly, needs an average monthly balance of $250 or more, US only. Because idle cash earns
the same 3.5%, interest is not a reason to hold "sure things": it is earned either way.

**Interest.** Kalshi pays 3.50% a year on cash and on positions alike (help.kalshi.com "APY on Kalshi", read 2026-11-18; variable;
$250 average monthly balance minimum; daily accrual, monthly payment; US only). Added to every bet it lifts the all-category test
result from -1.21% to -1.08% per $1 (13.7 days held on average), and the long-dated 97-99c rule from +1.6% to about +1.8%. Because
idle cash earns the same 3.5%, holding a 97c contract for a month earns its convergence (+1.6%, if it never loses) plus interest, while
cash earns the interest anyway: the interest is not an edge of the bet. Annualized, +1.6% a month looks like about 20% a year, but
only while nothing loses; one loss at 98c costs about 60 wins.

## Method (the honest version)

- **Data.** Kalshi's PUBLIC market data only (GET, no key, never an order; at most 1 request a second): the series list (with each
  series' fee multiplier), every settled market of 95 series picked by volume in 8 families (live `/markets` + `/historical/markets`,
  capped at the newest 4,000 + 4,000 per series), and yes bid / ask candles for a seeded random sample of their events (hourly over the
  last 7 days for markets that live 7 days or less, daily over the whole life otherwise).
- **Split, fixed before any outcome was read.** Old half (train) = markets Kalshi has moved to `/historical` (closed before
  2026-09-19). New half (held-out test, read once) = 2026-09-19 to 2026-11-18. Per series: 15 random old events (about 30 markets) and
  15 random new events (all their markets).
- **No look-ahead.** Two entry rules, both using only what was public before the order:
  1. *First touch*: the first candle after the open in which a side's ask (YES at the yes ask, NO at 1 - yes bid) sits in a price band,
     bought once per band per market. Nothing about the future is used.
  2. *Clock*: at 2 h, 8 h, 1 d, 3 d, 7 d, 21 d or 60 d before the SCHEDULED close. Markets that cannot close early: their close time.
     Markets that can (the event happening ends them): Kalshi's expected expiration time (the same for every market of an event and not
     moved by an early close; checked: YES and NO markets show the same pattern), shifted by the series' usual offset, and only for series
     where that offset is tight (median under 3 h). The real close is never used to time a trade, so a "sure thing" that died early is
     counted as a loss, not skipped. Mentions have no reliable clock (speeches end when they end): first touch only.
  The quote is the last candle that ended at or before the decision (at most 3 h old hourly, 36 h daily).
- **Costs.** Taker at the ask; spread at most 5c; fee 0.07 x the series' fee multiplier (Kalshi publishes 0.5 for some MLB series
  and 0 for a few long-dated ones) x C x P x (1-P), rounded UP to the cent on a $10 order (C = floor(10 / P)).
- **Bands.** 90-93c, 94-96c, 97-99c (the price paid).
- **Selection.** A cell (family x band x rule) is carried to the test only if its train cluster t (one cluster = one event) beats a
  one-sided Bonferroni bar over every eligible train cell (30+ trades, 8+ events). Pooled band x rule rows are pre-registered and
  shown whatever train says.
- **Interest.** Kalshi's help center (read 2026-11-18, "APY on Kalshi"): **3.50% APY, variable, on cash AND open positions**
  (positions valued at end-of-day last traded prices), accrues daily, paid monthly, needs an average monthly balance of at least
  $250, US users only, SSN needed above $10 a year. Added as cost x 3.5% x days held / 365.

## Honest limits

- Hourly or daily candle closes, not fills: a $10 order fills at the ask shown only if that much is offered; thin long-dated books
  could fill worse (that would make every number lower).
- Samples, not the whole population: 15 old + 15 new events per series (the last 22 series had at most 12 markets per new event,
  because the machine restarted three times). 1,141 train markets, 2,365 test markets, 7,564 trades in all.
- Politics: the big election series are one-off events (few clusters); the held-out half has only 10 politics trades, so politics is
  untested in practice, not "proven bad".
- The held-out half is two months (Sep 19 - Nov 18, 2026), one market regime; trades per month and $/month are scaled estimates.
- Mentions have no lookahead-free clock, so only first-touch was tested there.
