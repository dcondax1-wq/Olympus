# Caller onboarding pack (<Agency>, 2026-11-21)

Read this once (10 minutes). Questions: email <agency email>.

## 1. The rules (one page, no exceptions)

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Businesses only.** Call the number on the list, or a number a business shows on Google Maps. If the person says "this is my personal / home phone", apologise, log **Do not call**, hang up. | Home and personal numbers are protected by the federal Do-Not-Call rules; business lines are not. |
| **Say who you are first.** "Hi, it's <your name> with <Agency>." Never pretend to be someone else, a customer, Google, or "following up" on something that never happened. | Federal and Maryland law ban deceptive sales calls; it is also just decent. |
| **Dial by hand, talk live.** No auto-dialers, no "power dialer" apps, no pre-recorded messages, no voicemail-drop apps, no texts. | Maryland bans automated dialing or recorded messages for sales calls without signed consent: $500 per call (Md. Com. Law 14-4502). |
| **Never record a call.** Not with an app, not with a second phone. | Maryland needs every person's consent to record. Recording without it is a felony (Md. Courts & Jud. Proc. 10-402). |
| **Weekdays 9:00 am - 5:00 pm Eastern only.** No weekends, no holidays (the portal blocks them). | Maryland allows 8 am - 8 pm; we stay inside it with room to spare. |
| **1 call + 1 follow-up per business, never two calls the same day.** The follow-up comes 2+ business days later, and only after a voicemail or "call me back". | Not harassment. The portal counts it for you. |
| **"No" means never again.** "Not interested", "don't call", "take me off": say "Understood, sorry to bother you, have a good day", hang up, log **No** or **Do not call** that second. | It also stops our emails to them, forever. |
| **No lies, no pressure.** No fake deadlines ("only today"), no made-up clients, no "Google will penalise you". The site is free to look at; nothing is owed. | The FTC rule on business calls bans misrepresentations (16 CFR 310.3, B2B since 2024). |
| **Show a real number.** Call from your own phone or a free Google Voice number. Never hide your caller ID or fake another number. | Maryland and the FCC require a caller ID that can be called back. |
| **Only the agency's words about price.** Starter $1,500 + $79/month, or $149/month for 12 months with nothing upfront. No discounts or promises; "<Owner> will confirm that by email". | Only the owner sets prices and terms. |

## 2. The script (20 seconds to the question)

**Opener:**
"Hi, it's <your name> with <Agency>, a small web studio here in Maryland. Is this <Business>? ... I'll be quick: I noticed you don't
have a website, so we built one for you, free to look at. Can I email you the link?"

- **Yes** -> "Great, what's the best email?" (read it back, spell it). "You'll have it in two minutes. If you like it, I can book you
  10 minutes with <Owner> to make it yours: <day> at 10 or <day> at 2?" -> log **Interested** (or **Booked a call** with the time).
- **Who is this / what is this?** -> "<Agency> builds websites for local businesses. We picked <Business> because you have great reviews
  but no website, so we built a one-page site with your services, hours, tap-to-call and a map. It's yours to look at, no strings."
- **Not now** -> "No problem. Can I just email you the link so you have it?" (if no -> log **No**).

**Close:** "Thanks so much, <name>. The link is coming from <agency email>. Have a great day."

## 3. The 10 objections (one honest line each)

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| 1. "How much?" | "The starter site is $1,500 once plus $79 a month for hosting and small changes, or $149 a month for a year with nothing upfront. Can I email you the link and the prices together?" |
| 2. "We already have a website." | "Sorry, I didn't see one on Google. Could you tell me the address? ... Thanks, I'll make a note." (log **No**, note the address). |
| 3. "My nephew / a friend does that." | "That's great. The preview is already built, so it might give them ideas. Want me to email it anyway?" |
| 4. "I get my customers from word of mouth." | "That's the best kind. Most people still look you up on their phone before they call; the site is there for that moment. Want the link?" |
| 5. "I'm busy." | "I'll be quick: can I just email you the link to look at later?" If no: "No problem, have a good day." (log **Called** or **No**). |
| 6. "Is this a scam?" | "Fair question. Nothing to pay and nothing to sign. It's just a link to a site we built for you. If you don't like it, delete the email." |
| 7. "Send me something." | "Of course. What's the best email?" |
| 8. "I don't need a website." | "Totally fair. Thanks for your time, have a good day." (log **No**, never again). |
| 9. "Facebook is enough." | "Facebook is great. A site adds the Google search, the map and the tap-to-call. Can I email the preview so you can compare?" |
| 10. "Take me off your list" / "Don't call me." | "Understood, I've taken you off, and sorry to bother you. Have a good day." Hang up, log **Do not call**, never again. |

## 4. Voicemail (once only, live by you, never recorded in advance)

"Hi, this is <your name> with <Agency>, a small web studio here in Maryland. I'm calling <Business> because we built you a free website
preview, no strings. I'll email it if I can find your address, or you can call me back at <your number>. Thanks, have a good day."
Log **Voicemail**. The portal allows the one follow-up call 2+ business days later.

>
> <your name>, for <Agency>
> <Agency> · <postal address> · <agency phone>
> Not interested? Reply "no thanks" and we will never email or call again.

What makes it legal (CAN-SPAM, FTC): the real sender and business name, an honest subject line, the postal address, and an opt-out that is
honoured (we do it at once; the law allows 10 business days). The portal's **Email them the link** button fills all of this in for you.

## 6. Logging calls in the caller portal

1. Open the portal link the agency gives you and type **your name** (it goes on every call, for your commission) and the **passphrase**.
2. Today's list shows each business, its town, its code (like RO-127) and its concept site. If a row has no phone, find it on Google Maps
   (the business's own listing), type it in, and **wait until it shows on tomorrow's list** before you dial. The list is checked against the
   Do-Not-Call registry every night.
3. Tap **Call** (your phone dials it; you talk live). Then tap one result:
   **Talked** · **Voicemail** · **Interested** · **Booked a call** (with the time) · **No** · **Do not call** · **Won** (they paid).
4. **Email them the link** opens the agency's email with the template filled in.
5. At the end of each day tap **Send your log to the agency** (or **Save my log file** and email it to <agency email>). The agency imports it. "No" and "Do not call" then
   come off every list and every email for good, and your commission is counted.

If the list header says **"not scrubbed"** or **"STALE"**, don't dial. Email the agency.

## 7. Commission: how it is counted and paid (free tools only)

- **Your choice when you start:** $300 when a business you booked or emailed **pays**, or $40 for every **booked call that shows up**.
- **Counted from the portal log:** each Interested / Booked / Won line carries your name and the business code. The agency runs
  `python -m agency.portal commissions` and sends you the table every Friday (it lists both kinds; you are paid the one you chose; a booked call counts once the agency marks it "showed"). A sale counts for you if you logged that business within 30
  days before it paid.
- **Paid within 3 days of the client's payment**, by **Zelle** (free, bank to bank) or PayPal Friends & Family (free from a balance or a bank).
- **Taxes:** you are an independent contractor and pay your own. Send a W-9 before your first payment (free form at irs.gov). If you earn
  **$2,000 or more in 2026**, you get a 1099-NEC in January. That threshold rose from $600 under the 2025 tax law, and the agency files it
  free through the IRS's IRIS system.
